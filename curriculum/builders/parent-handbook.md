# Builders Parent Handbook
**Document ID:** CSA-BLD-PH-EN-v1.0 · **Program:** Builders (Grades 2–3) · Given to every family at enrolment.

---

## 1. Welcome to the CODEship Journey

Your child is a **Builder** — the second level of the CODEship Journey (Dream → Code → Achieve), which runs from Kindergarten to Grade 8. Builders move from blocks to *real code*: HTML structure and CSS styling, while developing informational and persuasive writing skills for the web.

By the end of the program, your Builder **builds and styles a real multi-page website** — presented at a family showcase.

Our promise: *Creativity Before Code.* No tutorials, no templates — real projects from your child's own ideas, with our tools, structure, and encouragement.

## 2. How the program works

- **4 semesters + a capstone**, about 20 sessions plus the capstone, each **55 minutes**.
- Every session: **Warm-up → Teach → Build → Reflect** — most of class is hands-on.
- **One real project per semester**, then the capstone: a multi-page website celebrating a helper in your community.
- **2 short quizzes per semester (8 total)** — read aloud, low-stakes, used to guide our teaching, never to rank children.
- Inclusive-design accommodations are built into every class; tell us what your child needs and we'll plan together before the first session.
- Writing is part of coding here: Builders deliberately strengthens the informational and persuasive writing your child is developing at school.

## 3. Semester roadmap

| Semester | Project | Your child learns |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | All About Me Page | HTML tags & page structure |
| 2 | Weather Helper | CSS styling & lists |
| 3 | Class Pet Care Guide | Links, multi-page sites & informational writing |
| 4 | Save the Bees Awareness Site | Persuasive writing & digital footprint |
| ★ | Community Helper Website (capstone) | Everything combined — a real multi-page site |

## 4. What you'll hear from us

Home Link notes at each semester's start and end (with a 10-minute, screen-free family activity) · rubric results plus 2–3 sentences of warm, specific feedback after every project · a showcase invitation about two weeks before the capstone.

## 5. Technology at home

**Nothing is required.** All building happens in class on our devices, in a browser-based editor with class-managed accounts. If your child wants to keep building at home, any free browser HTML editor works — we'll happily point you to what we use. 20–30 minutes at a time is plenty at this age.

Online-class families need a computer with a keyboard (tablets are hard for typing code), a browser, and a quiet spot. We help you test the setup before session one.

## 6. Questions to ask after class

- "What's a *tag*? Show me the tag hug." (Semester 1)
- "If our kitchen were a web page, what would the headings be?"
- "What did you style today — and why did you pick that colour?" (Semester 2)
- "Give me instructions for making toast — verb first!" (Semester 3)
- "Fact or opinion: 'our dog is the best dog'?" (Semester 4)
- Always: "What are you proud of building?"

## 7. Safety and your child's digital footprint

Builders publish only within the class environment. We teach and enforce safe-sharing rules: first name only, no addresses, schools, or phone numbers, class-approved images only, and — by Semester 4 — *true, kind, and safe* as the test for anything made public. You'll recognize these rules; feel free to use them at home.

## 8. Progress and showcase expectations

Steady progress looks like: recognizing tags → typing them independently → fixing their own broken pages with the "tag doctor" checklist → writing for a real audience → presenting confidently. The capstone showcase (final session) is a family event: your Builder presents their own multi-page website live, and certificates are presented there.

## 9. Practical notes

Drop-off/pickup with roster check (in person); online classes admit children from the waiting room by roster name. Accommodation updates and questions any time — at pickup or via your location lead.
