# Builders Project Manual
**Document ID:** CSA-BLD-PM-EN-v1.0 · **Program:** Builders (Grades 2–3)

Standard template per CSA-STD-PUB §8; rubrics in CSA-BLD-AM-EN §6–7.

---

## Project 1 — All About Me Page (Semester 1)

- **Overview:** a structured personal web page — real HTML — with a main heading, two or more sections, and an image with alt text.
- **Objectives / outcomes:** tags; structure; safe sharing on the web.
- **Required skills by build day:** h1/h2/p (S1.2), img+alt (S1.3), skeleton & nesting (S1.4).
- **Materials:** class editor + `about-me` starter; wireframe planner; safe-sharing checklist; class image folder.
- **Timeline:** seeded S1.1 → skills S1.2–S1.4 → published S1.5.
- **Teacher guide:** the emotional hook is authorship — "a page about YOU, by YOU, in the real language of the web." Enforce the safe-sharing checklist as a proud professional step, not a restriction. Keep typing loads small; starter files carry boilerplate.
- **Student instructions:** "Build a page that introduces you: one big heading, two sections with their own headings and stories, one picture with alt text. First name only — web authors protect their private info."
- **Extensions:** third section; `<strong>` emphasis; a favourite-things list (previews S2).
- **Presentation guide:** device gallery walk + 3-line tour ("My page has… My favourite tag is… I'm proud of…").
- **Example outcomes:** "All About Maya" (Things I Love / My Hockey Life); "About Sam" (My Family / Dinosaur Facts).
- **Common challenges:** unclosed tags swallowing the page (tag-doctor); oversharing (address, school) — checklist catches it; perfectionism about looks — "style is next semester's superpower."

## Project 2 — Weather Helper (Semester 2)

- **Overview:** a styled reference page that helps someone dress right for the weather — seasonal sections with checklists, each styled to match its mood.
- **Objectives / outcomes:** CSS styling; lists; classes; style serving information.
- **Required skills:** rules & properties (S2.1), ul/ol/li (S2.2), classes (S2.3).
- **Materials:** `weather` starters; CSS property cards; list sort cards.
- **Timeline:** style S2.1 → lists S2.2 → classes S2.3 → assembled S2.4 → demoed S2.5.
- **Teacher guide:** anchor every style choice to *use*: "does the snowy section FEEL cold? Can a reader find the rainy list fast?" Restraint rule (2–3 colours) prevents rainbow soup. Weather is universally relatable — invite local weather stories.
- **Student instructions:** "Build a helper page for 2+ kinds of weather. Each gets a heading, a what-to-wear list, and its own mood style. Someone should be able to glance and get dressed right."
- **Extensions:** third season; styled list items; a "today's weather" banner class.
- **Presentation guide:** demo + "I styled it to feel ___ because ___" (design intent is the assessed insight).
- **Example outcomes:** Snowy/Sunny helper with icy blues vs warm yellows; four-season page with numbered "get ready" routines.
- **Common challenges:** missing semicolons (read the rule aloud); ul/ol confusion (order test: "does the order matter?"); style overload (squint test).

## Project 3 — Class Pet Care Guide (Semester 3)

- **Overview:** a real multi-page website (Home / Feeding / Play & Care) that a pet-sitter could genuinely follow, with working navigation.
- **Objectives / outcomes:** links; multi-page structure; informational writing for a real audience.
- **Required skills:** a/href round-trips (S3.2), verb-first numbered instructions (S3.3), nav assembly (S3.4).
- **Materials:** `pet-guide` starter (multi-file); fact organizers; writing frames; link checklist.
- **Timeline:** facts S3.1 → links S3.2 → writing S3.3 → assembled S3.4 → handed over S3.5.
- **Teacher guide:** audience is everything — recruit a real "pet-sitter" guest for handover day if at all possible (office staff, parent volunteer, another teacher 💻). The robot-instructions demo (S3.3) is the writing lesson kids remember; play it fully. Class pet may be real or invented by vote — invented pets need consistent invented facts.
- **Student instructions:** "Write and build a guide a pet-sitter could really use: Home page about our pet, Feeding page with numbered steps, Play & Care page with what they need. Every page links to the others."
- **Extensions:** fourth page (Fun Facts); photos per page with alt; emergency-contact section (school-appropriate, no private data).
- **Presentation guide:** handover framing: "If you looked after our pet, my guide shows you ___" + one genuine guest question.
- **Example outcomes:** hamster guide (real class pet); invented axolotl guide with voted-on facts.
- **Common challenges:** href/filename mismatches (copy-paste rule); instructions written to self not audience (partner can't-do-it test); scope creep to five pages (site map caps at 3–4).

## Project 4 — Save the Bees Awareness Site (Semester 4)

- **Overview:** a persuasive one-page (stretch: two-page) campaign site: headline, checked facts, deliberate design, action list, and call to action.
- **Objectives / outcomes:** persuasive writing; design with intent; digital footprint (publishing responsibly).
- **Required skills:** fact vs. opinion + headlines (S4.1), mood styling (S4.2), CTA + footprint check (S4.3).
- **Materials:** bee fact sheets; fact/opinion cards; `bees` starter; action ideas sheet.
- **Timeline:** research S4.1 → design S4.2 → action S4.3 → assembled S4.4 → launched S4.5.
- **Teacher guide:** hold the fact/opinion line firmly — persuasion at CODEship is facts + feelings, never made-up claims (this is the media-literacy seed for Developers). Design conversations should always route through the message: "does that colour help bees, or just look cool?"
- **Student instructions:** "Make people CARE about bees: a headline that grabs, three true facts, a design that feels right, and tell your reader exactly what to do. Before launch: is everything true, kind, and safe?"
- **Extensions:** second page of facts with nav; styled highlight class for key words; class "hive" page linking all campaign sites.
- **Presentation guide:** 30-second campaign speech; audience names "one thing I'll actually do" — persuasion measured by response.
- **Example outcomes:** "Bees feed you. Feed them back." with plant-a-flower CTA; "No Bees, No Berries" with three-action list.
- **Common challenges:** invented "facts" (source line required on the collector); guilt-based messaging (steer to hopeful action); CTA too vague ("help bees" → "plant one flower this spring").

## Capstone — Community Helper Website

- **Overview:** a real multi-page website celebrating a helper in the child's own community — the program's headline outcome made visible.
- **Objectives / outcomes:** independent integration of tags, structure, CSS, lists, links, and both writing modes.
- **Requirements:** real local helper; 2+ pages linked both ways; deliberate styling; page one informs, page two persuades; safe-sharing gate passed.
- **Timeline:** planned + page one C.1 → completed, rehearsed, showcased C.2.
- **Teacher guide:** the helper must be real and local — this grounds authenticity and community connection (a brand pillar). Idea cards for the stuck, never assignments. Where consent and family logistics allow, deliver the site to the helper after showcase — the thank-you effect is the memory that lasts.
- **Student instructions:** "Choose a real person who helps your community. Build them a website: who they are, and why they matter. Make it so good they'd be proud to see it."
- **Presentation guide:** family showcase; frame: "My site celebrates… Watch what happens when I click… I'm proud of…"; certificates follow (AM §9).
- **Example outcomes:** crossing-guard tribute site; library-branch celebration; "Coach Dee Changed Our Team".
- **Common challenges:** choosing celebrities over local helpers (steer to someone they've met); two-page requirement wobbles (site map planner front-loads it); shy presenters (pair rehearsal + frame cards).
