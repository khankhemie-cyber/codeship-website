# CODEship Academy Classroom Operations Manual
**Document ID:** CSA-STD-OPS-EN-v1.0 · **Status:** Approved for production · **Applies to:** every CODEship classroom, in-person and online

---

## 1. Purpose

This manual standardizes how a CODEship classroom runs — setup, equipment, student management, safety, flow, and end-of-class procedures — so that a family walking into any CODEship location, or logging into any CODEship online class, has the same high-quality experience.

## 2. Classroom setup standards

### In-person

- **Room ready 30 minutes before the first class** (8:30 AM for the Saturday block).
- One device per student, charged/powered, with the session's tool already loaded and tested on the instructor machine.
- Seating in pods of 2–4 facing the demonstration screen; every seat must see the screen without turning more than 90°.
- Posted at child height: the visual schedule (Warm-up → Teach → Build → Reflect), the class agreements poster, and the current semester's project wall.
- Brand environment: bright, clean, welcoming. No dark/gamer aesthetics, no rank or belt displays. Student work is displayed with first names only.

### Online

- Instructor camera on, background clean or branded, logged in 10 minutes early.
- Session tool links pre-posted in the class channel; a co-browse/screen-share test performed before students arrive.
- Waiting room enabled; students admitted by roster name only.
- 💻 Online classes follow the same four-phase flow; Build-phase support happens via screen-share rotations.

## 3. Equipment and technology standards

| Item | Standard |
|------|----------|
| Student devices | Explorers: tablets or desktops with touch/mouse; Builders and up: keyboard devices (laptops/desktops) |
| Instructor station | Laptop + projector/large display; can mirror any student's screen category |
| Internet | Wired or reliable Wi-Fi; offline fallback activities packed for every session (→ each program's Printable Resource Book) |
| Accounts | Class-managed accounts only; students never create personal accounts or share personal data in class tools |
| Spares | Minimum one spare device per 8 students; power bars taped and cable-managed |

## 4. The Saturday flow (in-person)

All five cities run the same agenda: Explorers 9:00–9:55 · transition 15 min · Builders 10:10–11:05 · break 35 min · Developers 11:40–12:35 · transition 15 min · Engineers 12:50–1:45.

**Transitions (15 min):** previous class dismissed to guardians at the door by roster check-off; devices reset (previous work saved/closed, session tool reloaded); seating and materials reset; next roster staged.

**Break (35 min):** instructor resets the room for the older-grades afternoon; equipment charging rotated; incident notes (if any) written while fresh.

## 5. Student management and behaviour expectations

Class agreements, taught in session 1.1 of every semester and posted always:

1. **We are kind** — to people, in person and online.
2. **We try, test, and try again** — bugs are part of building.
3. **We take care of the equipment** — hands know the rules.
4. **We share the room** — voices low during Build, everyone presents, everyone listens.

Behaviour approach: positive-first (catch students building well), redirect privately, never punitive in public. Escalation path: quiet redirect → seat/partner change → break with instructor → note to guardian at pickup → location lead conversation with family. Safety issues skip straight to the location lead.

## 6. Accommodations protocol

Families flag needs at registration or any time after ("Let us know about any specific accommodations your child needs, and our team will work with you before the first session" — public promise).

1. Accommodation notes appear on the class roster before session 1.
2. Instructor confirms the plan with the family at first drop-off (or by message online).
3. In-class toolkit (always available, no diagnosis required): flexible pacing variants in every Build phase, oral+visual instructions, movement breaks, seating choice, reduced-stimulation corner, extended time on quizzes, scribe or oral responses for written tasks.
4. Never single a child out publicly for an accommodation.

## 7. Safety procedures

- Roster check-in/check-out; children released only to listed adults (in person). Online: students admitted from waiting room by roster name; recordings per location policy and parental consent only.
- Two-adult visibility standard: no instructor alone with a single child out of sight of others.
- Electrical/equipment: cables managed, liquids away from devices, no student handles power connections at Explorers/Builders age.
- Digital safety in class mirrors the curriculum: class accounts only, no personal information in projects (first names only), image use per consent forms.
- Incident reports written the same day and filed with the location lead.

## 8. End-of-class procedures

1. Reflect phase ends with next-session goal recorded in workbooks.
2. Students save work to the class account and shut down per the device routine.
3. Dismissal by roster; instructor shares one positive, specific note with as many guardians as possible at pickup (rotating so every family hears one at least twice a semester).
4. Room reset; equipment counted and charged; instructor logs attendance, quiz scores (if quiz day), and any notes within 24 hours.

## 9. Instructor quality standards

- Knows every student's name by session 2 and something they're building by session 3.
- Follows the session plan's four-phase timing; Build is never sacrificed to a long Teach.
- Uses the warm-feedback protocol ("I like… / I wonder…") and models it before students use it.
- Never codes *for* a student; guides with questions ("What do you want it to do? What is it doing instead?").
- Fidelity: teaches the published sequence; enrichment goes in the extension slots, not in place of core content (→ CSA-STD-FDS-EN §3).
