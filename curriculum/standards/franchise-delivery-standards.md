# CODEship Academy Franchise Delivery Standards Manual
**Document ID:** CSA-STD-FDS-EN-v1.0 · **Status:** Approved for production · **Applies to:** every current and future CODEship Academy location worldwide

---

## 1. Purpose

This manual defines what must be identical at every CODEship Academy location — so a family in Toronto, Vancouver, Calgary, a new franchise city, or an online class in Guyana receives the same program, the same quality, and the same brand experience. It is the franchise-facing companion to the Curriculum Framework (CSA-STD-CF) and Classroom Operations Manual (CSA-STD-OPS).

## 2. The non-negotiables (protected core)

Every location must deliver, without local modification:

1. **The four-level CODEship Journey** — Explorers (K–1, visual blocks), Builders (2–3, HTML/CSS), Developers (4–5, JavaScript), Engineers (6–8, Python + AI + cybersecurity).
2. **Program structure** — 4 semesters + capstone per program; 5 sessions per semester; 55-minute sessions; 2 quizzes per semester.
3. **The session model** — Warm-up (5) → Teach (10) → Build (30) → Reflect (10).
4. **The published projects and learning outcomes** — semester projects, Big Ideas, and skills as printed in each Master Curriculum Manual.
5. **The assessment system** — 8 quizzes, 4-level project rubrics, warm-feedback protocol, parent reporting cadence.
6. **Inclusion standards** — accommodations toolkit available in every class (→ CSA-STD-OPS §6).
7. **Brand voice and visual identity** — including "Dream. Code. Achieve.", "Creativity Before Code", the accent-colour system, and the clean/inclusive design rule (no dark aesthetics, no gamer culture, no belt systems).
8. **Alignment posture** — provincial-curriculum claims only as published, always with the non-endorsement disclaimer.

## 3. Lesson fidelity standard

- Instructors teach the published session sequence. Skipping, reordering, or replacing sessions requires head-office curriculum approval.
- **Where local flavour belongs:** the extension activities, project theming (a student's neighbourhood map maps *their* neighbourhood), showcase formats, and community examples. Localization enriches the Build; it never replaces the Teach.
- If a session is missed (holiday, closure), the location uses the compression guidance in the program's Instructor Quick Reference Guide rather than improvising cuts.

## 4. Technology substitution rule

Locations may substitute tools within the platform categories defined in CSA-STD-CF §10 **only if** every skill in the program's skills matrix remains teachable and student work remains portable across locations. Substitutions are registered with head office so support materials stay accurate.

## 5. Experience standards

**Student experience:** every student presents work at least once per semester; every student receives warm, specific instructor feedback on every project; no student's work is ranked against another's.

**Parent experience:** parents receive — a Parent Handbook at enrolment; a mid-semester check-in; end-of-semester rubric results with 2–3 sentences of specific feedback; an invitation to every showcase. Pickup interactions follow the positive-note rotation (→ CSA-STD-OPS §8).

**Classroom experience:** room/online setup, safety, and behaviour standards exactly per CSA-STD-OPS.

## 6. Instructor onboarding and quality assurance

- New instructors complete: framework orientation (CSA-STD-CF) → program Teacher Manual study → co-teach minimum 2 sessions → observed solo session against the observation checklist below.
- **Observation checklist (pass = all yes):** four-phase timing held · names used · guided with questions, never coded for a student · warm-feedback protocol used · inclusion toolkit visible in practice · room/tech standards met · accurate content.
- Each location is observed at least once per semester by the franchise owner or head office; results feed the location's quality file.

## 7. Assessment consistency

- Quizzes administered as published (sessions x.3 and x.5), scored with the Assessment Manual keys, recorded within 24 hours.
- Rubric calibration: once per semester, instructors at each location co-score one anonymized sample project against the rubric and reconcile differences.
- Certificates issue only on the published completion criteria (→ each program's Assessment Manual §9).

## 8. Brand and marketing consistency

- Locations use head-office-published program descriptions verbatim; local pages may add schedule, venue, and instructor bios only.
- Public claims about outcomes, alignment, session counts, and pricing must match the website's single source of truth. Any discrepancy is reported to head office, not "fixed" locally.
- Photography and student work sharing per consent forms; first names only.

## 9. Operational consistency

- Enrolment, scheduling, and payment through the head-office registration platform.
- The Saturday four-program block (youngest first, one-visit drop-off/pickup) is the default in-person pattern for new locations; deviations require head-office scheduling approval.
- Attendance, quiz records, incident reports, and semester reports on head-office templates (→ Printable Resource Books).

## 10. Quality assurance cycle

| Cadence | Activity |
|---------|----------|
| Every session | Instructor log: attendance, notes, (quiz scores on quiz days) |
| Every semester | Location observation · rubric calibration · parent-experience pulse (2-question survey at showcase) |
| Every year | Full location audit against this manual · curriculum feedback submitted to head office for the library's annual minor revision |
