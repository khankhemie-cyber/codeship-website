# Developers Parent Handbook
**Document ID:** CSA-DEV-PH-EN-v1.0 · **Program:** Developers (Grades 4–5) · Given to every family at enrolment.

---

## 1. Welcome to the CODEship Journey

Your child is a **Developer** — the third level of the CODEship Journey (Dream → Code → Achieve), arriving at the **Code** arc. Developers write real JavaScript — variables, functions, conditionals, and loops — while building tools that solve everyday problems, and learning media and financial literacy along the way.

By the end of the program, your Developer **builds interactive tools that solve real problems** — including a capstone app that solves a problem from their own life, presented at a family showcase.

Our promise: *Creativity Before Code.* No tutorials, no templates — your child's projects run on their own ideas, thresholds, and words.

## 2. How the program works

- **4 semesters + a capstone**, about 20 sessions plus the capstone, each **55 minutes**.
- Every session: **Warm-up → Teach → Build → Reflect** — most of class is hands-on building.
- **One real tool built per semester**, then the capstone Problem-Solver App.
- **2 short quizzes per semester (8 total)** — low-stakes checkpoints that guide our teaching; never rankings.
- Inclusive-design accommodations built into every class; flag your child's needs any time and we'll plan together.
- **Cross-curricular by design:** Semester 3 teaches media literacy (how to check sources) and Semester 4 teaches financial literacy (budgets, needs vs. wants) — through code.

## 3. Semester roadmap

| Semester | Project | Your child learns |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | Homework Timer & Focus Tool | Variables, functions, events |
| 2 | Healthy Snack Calculator | Conditionals — code that decides |
| 3 | Fact or Fake? Media-Literacy Quiz | Arrays, loops & how to question sources |
| 4 | Budget Buddy | Loops, conditionals & money smarts |
| ★ | Problem-Solver App (capstone) | Everything combined, on a problem they chose |

## 4. What you'll hear from us

Home Link notes at each semester's start and end (with a 10-minute family activity — the Semester 3 dinner fact-check game is a favourite) · rubric results plus 2–3 sentences of warm, specific feedback after every project · showcase invitation about two weeks before the capstone.

## 5. Technology at home

**Nothing is required.** All coding happens in class in a browser-based playground on class accounts. If your Developer wants to build at home, the same kind of free browser playground works anywhere — we'll point you to it on request. Around 30 minutes at a time is plenty.

Online-class families need a computer with a keyboard, a browser, and a quiet spot; we test the setup with you before session one.

## 6. Questions to ask after class

- "What's a variable? I heard it's a box…" (Semester 1)
- "Show me the chain: what happens when you click the button?"
- "What does your calculator say about my granola bar?" (Semester 2)
- "Is this headline real? How would you check?" (Semester 3 — expect the three questions: who made it, what's the evidence, what do others say)
- "What does your Budget Buddy yell when I overspend?" (Semester 4)
- Always: "What was your best bug this week?" (Bugs are trophies here — ask to see the bug log.)

## 7. About the money and media content

Financial literacy uses kid-scale scenarios (class-trip snacks, a bike fund) — we never ask about or discuss family finances. Media literacy uses age-safe topics (animals, space, food records) — the skill we teach is the checking habit, and every "fact" in your child's quiz is verified with a source note.

## 8. Progress and showcase expectations

Steady progress looks like: explaining what a variable holds → tracing what code will do before running it → reading error messages as clues → building end-to-end tools → pitching a solution to a real problem. The capstone showcase (final session) is a family event: your Developer demos their own app and tells you what they'd build next. Certificates are presented there.

## 9. Practical notes

Drop-off/pickup with roster check (in person); online classes admit from the waiting room by roster name. Accommodation updates and questions any time via your instructor or location lead.
