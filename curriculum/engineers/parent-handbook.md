# Engineers Parent Handbook
**Document ID:** CSA-ENG-PH-EN-v1.0 · **Program:** Engineers (Grades 6–8) · Given to every family at enrolment.

---

## 1. Welcome to the CODEship Journey

Your child is an **Engineer** — the final level of the CODEship Journey (Dream → Code → Achieve). Engineers write Python, train and evaluate their own AI models with an eye on bias and ethics, analyze real data, and learn practical cybersecurity.

By the end of the program, your Engineer **codes in Python, trains an AI responsibly, and pitches a real tech solution** — a prototype addressing a UN Sustainable Development Goal, presented live at Pitch Day.

Our promise: *Creativity Before Code.* Real problems, real tools, real stakes — and every project genuinely your child's own.

## 2. How the program works

- **4 semesters + a capstone**, about 20 sessions plus the capstone, each **55 minutes**.
- Every session: **Warm-up → Teach → Build → Reflect** — most of class is hands-on.
- **One substantial project per semester**, then the capstone Innovation Challenge.
- **2 short quizzes per semester (8 total)** — low-stakes checkpoints for our teaching, never rankings.
- Inclusive-design accommodations built into every class; flag needs any time.
- **AI is taught with ethics inside it, not alongside it:** your child will train a model, deliberately expose its bias, fix it, and audit their own chatbot for who it helps and who it could harm.

## 3. Semester roadmap

| Semester | Project | Your child learns |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | Password Strength Checker | Python logic & real cybersecurity |
| 2 | Smart Sorting AI | Training & evaluating an AI model; bias & ethics |
| 3 | Chatbot for Good | Responsible AI, built and audited |
| 4 | Data Detective | Real data analysis, honest charts |
| ★ | Innovation Challenge (capstone) | Everything combined: an SDG prototype, pitched |

## 4. What you'll hear from us

Home Link notes at each semester's start and end · rubric results plus 2–3 sentences of warm, specific feedback after every project · a **Pitch Day invitation** about two weeks before the capstone — families attend, and where possible a small community panel joins.

## 5. About the safety rails (please reinforce at home)

- **Passwords:** we teach password strength with test strings only — your child will never type or share a real password in class, and we ask the same discipline at home. The Semester 1 take-home safety audit is private to your family; it's a great conversation starter.
- **Cybersecurity = defence:** we teach how attacks work conceptually so kids can protect themselves — never how to attack.
- **Data & AI:** no personal data ever goes into models, bots, or datasets. Class datasets are curated, real, and age-safe.

## 6. Technology at home

**Nothing is required.** Everything runs in class on browser-based tools with class accounts. Engineers who want to build at home can use the same kind of free browser Python environment — we'll point you to it on request. Online-class families need a computer with keyboard, browser, and a quiet spot; we test the setup together before session one.

## 7. Questions to ask after class

- "How do accounts actually get hacked? Not the movie version." (Semester 1)
- "How did your AI get biased — and how did you fix it?" (Semester 2)
- "Who does your bot help? Who could it hurt?" (Semester 3)
- "What's your headline — and what's your caveat?" (Semester 4)
- "Which SDG did you pick, and why that one?" (capstone)
- Always: "What did you prove today?" (Their mission log answers exactly this.)

## 8. Progress and Pitch Day expectations

Steady progress looks like: reading error messages calmly → testing claims with evidence → naming a model's limits honestly → presenting with real terminology → pitching a scoped solution with a live demo and an honest "what's next". Pitch Day (final session) is a family event; certificates are presented there. Expect to be impressed by the *caveats* — honest limits are what we grade highest.

## 9. Practical notes

Drop-off/pickup with roster check (in person); online classes admit from the waiting room by roster name. Accommodation updates and questions any time via your instructor or location lead. The Engineers curriculum is also offered in French — ask your location about Québec/French-language availability.
